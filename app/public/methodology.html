How this site works

Every ranking here is computed from two things: prices read off Bangladeshi shop listings, and what published reviewers and owners said about the phone. This page is the whole method, including what it cannot tell you. Last data check 9 Sep 2026.

Built and run by Arkanullah Saad. Nothing here is for sale — not a position in a list, not a link, not a mention.

Where the prices come from

17 Bangladeshi sellers, re-read every day or two.

We read the public listings of Apple Gadgets, Dazzle, Excel Store, Excel Tech, Executive Machines, GadgetGear, Gazi Electronics, OPPO Bangladesh, Pickaboo, realme Bangladesh, Rio International, Salextra, SMSGadget, Sumash Tech, Tecno Bangladesh, vivo Bangladesh and Xiaomi Bangladesh. Each read records the price, the configuration it buys, the channel, whether the shop says it is in stock, and when we saw it. Nothing is typed in by hand and nothing is estimated: a figure no shop published does not appear.

What the words on a price mean

  • A price is the number one shop published for one exact configuration — that RAM, that storage, that market. A phone's headline range is the lowest and highest of those within one channel, never a low from one channel beside a high from another.
  • In stock means the shop said so on our last read. A shop that has never reported stock is recorded as unknown, not as out of stock: an absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
  • No price is its own state. Some phones in the catalogue have never been listed by any shop we read, and those pages say exactly that. They do not show a zero, invent a last price, or call the phone sold out.
  • Official and unofficial are different products. Official is a unit with a Bangladesh manufacturer warranty; unofficial is an import. Their prices are never averaged together.

How a phone gets onto a guide

The gate runs before anything is ranked.

Every one of these, or it is not on the page at all

  • A specification sheet matched to this exact handset, not to a same-named variant sold elsewhere.
  • A current price from a shop we read directly, confirmed in stock, with the shop named on the page.
  • Inside the guide's price band: no lower than 55% of the budget, and no higher than the ceiling the guide's title names. A pick above the ceiling is labelled a stretch with the gap stated in taka, and it is never described as fitting.
  • Released within the last 4 years, and not superseded by a newer phone in the same line selling for about the same money.

Anything that fails is not ranked lower. It is absent, and if that empties a guide the guide says so rather than reaching for a phone the gate rejected.

How the scores are built

A percentile inside our own catalogue, not a verdict about the world.

A score of 78 means ahead of 78% of the phones we track on that capability. That is a claim the data can back, unlike “7.8 out of 10 for camera”, which is a claim about photography. It also re-calibrates itself: as the catalogue moves to faster panels and bigger batteries, yesterday's 80 slides down on its own with nothing to re-tune.

Each capability blends the measured specification with what owners and reviewers said about that same capability. Specifications lead where a number really settles it, such as silicon and refresh rate. People lead where a spec sheet is known to mislead, such as battery life and how photos come out. A capability with too little evidence behind it is left blank rather than guessed, and a phone with too few scored capabilities gets no overall at all.

These scores are not published as a star rating in our search markup. They are assembled from other people's reviews, and presenting someone else's average as our own review rating would misrepresent where it came from.

What this method cannot tell you

The honest limits

  • We have not held the phone. Nothing here is a hands-on test, and where a page reasons from a specification it says so.
  • A price is what a shop published when we last read it, not a quote. Shops change prices faster than any crawler and stock moves within the day.
  • We read the shops we read. A phone may be cheaper somewhere we do not track, and a page can only ever describe our own coverage.
  • Owner reports are self-selected. People post when something goes wrong far more often than when nothing does, and the scores inherit that.

When we get it wrong

Tell our Facebook page the phone and the shop and it is fixed on the next data run. Corrections entered by hand outrank anything the pipeline derived and survive every later rebuild, so a re-run cannot quietly undo them.

What people ask about the method

Who writes these recommendations?

Bhalo Phone is one person's project, built and run by Arkanullah Saad. The ranking is computed, not hand-placed, and the prose around it is generated from the evidence each page lists and checked against that same evidence before it publishes. No shop or brand has ever been offered a position.

Do you test the phones yourselves?

No, and the site does not claim to. What we do is read what other people published — reviewer write-ups, video reviews and owner reports — and turn it into per-capability scores whose basis you can see. Every page names the sources it drew on. Where a claim rests on a specification rather than a measurement, the page says so instead of dressing an estimate up as a test.

What does “official” mean on a price?

An official listing is a unit sold with a Bangladesh manufacturer warranty. An unofficial one is an import: usually cheaper, usually without that warranty, sometimes built for another market's bands. They are different products at different prices, so we never merge them into one number. A price always carries the channel it belongs to, and where both exist the page shows both.

Why do you not link to the shops?

Because the moment a link earns money the ranking has a reason to move, and you have no way to tell whether it did. We name the shop, the configuration, the channel and the day we saw the price, which is enough to find the listing without us being paid to send you there.

A price here is wrong. What now?

Tell our Facebook page which phone and which shop, and it is corrected on the next data run. Shops move prices faster than any crawler, so a wrong price is usually a stale one rather than a mistake of judgement — either way the fix is the same, and a correction entered by hand outranks anything the pipeline derived.
