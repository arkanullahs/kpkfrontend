Is the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 worth buying?

What is good

  • Stunning 2600 nits foldable display
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite performance
  • 200 MP main camera with OIS
  • Fast 45W charging

What is not

  • Battery life only average
  • Visible crease worsens over time
  • Only gray import stock, no brand warranty

Buy it if Buy if you want flagship performance, a large foldable screen, and can accept a shop warranty.

Skip it if Skip if you need long battery endurance or an official Samsung warranty.

Why we say that

The 8.0 inch Dynamic LTPO AMOLED 2X screen reaches 2600 nits and runs at 120 Hz, making everything look bright and smooth. Snapdragon 8 Elite and 12 GB RAM give you top‑tier performance for gaming and multitasking, reflected in a 9.8 performance rating. The 200 MP main sensor with f/1.7 aperture and OIS captures detailed photos, earning an 8.5 camera score.

Battery life is only average; the 4400 mAh cell gets a 6 out of 10 rating, so heavy use will need a midday charge. The most common complaints are the visible crease that can become more pronounced with time and the lack of an official Samsung warranty because only gray import stock is available. At an unofficial price starting from 184,999 taka, it is a premium purchase that feels rewarding if you accept these trade‑offs.

Marques Brownlee · Samsung Z Fold 8 (Wide) Impressions: Better Than I Thought! ↗

Marques Brownlee's initial impressions are highly positive, noting that Samsung's new 'passport' shaped Fold 8 is a clever strategic move against Apple. He praises the device's extreme thinness and lightness, comparing it favorably to a regular smartphone. While acknowledging it lacks the telephoto lens and massive multitasking space of the Ultra, he finds the squatter aspect ratios vastly superior for casual media consumption, photography, and reading, making it a surprisingly compelling daily driver.

Marques Brownlee · Galaxy Z Fold 8 Review: Honeymoon's Over ↗

In his full review, Marques Brownlee balances his initial love for the Fold 8's hardware with the practical realities of using it. He reiterates that the wider cover screen and 4:3 inner screen make it a joy to hold and use for most tasks. However, the 'honeymoon is over' as he highlights significant app compatibility issues caused by the unusual screen shapes, the annoying placement of the speakers when unfolded, and the mediocre performance of the cameras given the $1,900 price tag.

Tech Spurt · Samsung Z Fold 8 Review | Get this, not the Ultra ↗

Tech Spurt rigorously tested the Fold 8 during a trip to Disneyland and came away highly impressed by its practical design. He loved that the wider cover screen allowed him to comfortably handle messages and emails without needing to unfold the device. While he found the battery life surprisingly capable and the durability reassuring, he was disappointed by the lack of S Pen support, the absence of a telephoto camera, and the phone's tendency to overheat and stutter during intensive gaming sessions.

GSMArena Official · Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 vs. Z Fold 8 Ultra: Which one should you get? ↗

GSMArena compared the Z Fold 8 directly against the Z Fold 8 Ultra, finding that the choice largely comes down to form factor preference rather than raw specs. They noted the Fold 8's cover screen is better for reading, while the Ultra's is better for scrolling and typing. Surprisingly, the smaller Fold 8 won their battery tests. However, the Ultra clearly outperformed the standard Fold 8 in camera quality (especially zoom and video) and charging speed, leaving buyers to weigh those features against the Fold 8's more compact design.
