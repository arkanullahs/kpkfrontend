Is the Honor Magic 8 worth buying?

What is good

  • 7000 mAh battery gives exceptional endurance
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite delivers flagship performance
  • 6.58" LTPO OLED with 120 Hz and high brightness
  • 100 W fast charging refills quickly

What is not

  • USB 2.0 Type‑C port feels outdated
  • No eSIM support
  • Camera underwhelms for flagship price
  • Software includes bloat and ads

Buy it if Buy it if you need long battery life, love heavy gaming, and value fast charging above camera excellence.

Skip it if Skip it if you rely on top‑tier photography, need eSIM, or want a brand warranty.

Why we say that

The 7000 mAh battery really shines, giving you more than a full day of mixed use even with a bright 6.58 inch LTPO OLED screen that can hit 6000 nits at peak. Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 paired with 12 GB RAM makes every app feel instant and gaming runs at high frame rates. The 100 W charger refills the huge cell quickly, and the IR blaster adds a handy remote control option.

However the camera sensor feels ordinary for the price, low‑light shots are soft and the software includes noticeable bloat and ads. The phone only has a USB 2.0 Type‑C port and lacks eSIM support, which feels outdated. Warranty comes from the shop, not Honor, so verify IMEI and BTRC status before buying.

The reviews

Via Tech ↗

Via Tech found the Honor Magic 8 Standard Edition to be a remarkably well-rounded device with no obvious shortcomings. They praised its comfortable 6.58-inch flat screen, massive battery, and the inclusion of fast wireless charging. The reviewer was particularly impressed by the software, highlighting the versatile new physical AI button and the system's unique ability to seamlessly clone extensive data from both iOS and HarmonyOS devices. Ultimately, they highly recommend it for anyone who doesn't need absolute top-tier professional photography hardware.

TechXReviews · Honor Magic 8 Review: Why Is No One Talking About This? ↗

TechXReviews highly praised the Honor Magic 8 for its exceptional battery efficiency and robust gaming performance, noting that it effortlessly maintains high frame rates with excellent stability. The reviewer loved the display, pointing out its class-leading eye protection features. While they felt the physical main camera sensor was a bit underwhelming for a modern flagship, they were incredibly impressed by Honor's new image processing algorithms, which produce highly realistic, natural photos without aggressive digital sharpening. They concluded it is a highly competitive, overlooked option.
