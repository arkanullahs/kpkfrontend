Is the OnePlus 13s worth buying?

What is good

  • Snapdragon 8 Elite handles games effortlessly
  • 1600‑nit LTPO AMOLED stays vivid outdoors
  • 5850 mAh battery lasts a full day easily
  • 80 W charger refills in under an hour

What is not

  • No ultrawide lens, only 2× telephoto
  • No IP68 water resistance or wireless charging
  • Camera quality drops after software updates
  • Only shop warranty, no brand support

Buy it if Buy if you want flagship performance and a bright display at a lower price and can accept a modest camera and no official warranty.

Skip it if Skip if you need a reliable camera, water resistance, or an official manufacturer warranty.

Why we say that

It runs on the Snapdragon 8 Elite and 12 GB RAM, so apps launch instantly and games stay smooth at 120 Hz. The 6.32‑inch LTPO AMOLED stays vivid even in bright sunlight and the 5850 mAh battery easily lasts a full day of mixed use, while the 80 W charger tops up quickly. The 50 MP main sensor looks decent in good light, yet owners complain about inconsistent processing, noisy images after software updates, and the lack of an ultrawide lens makes the camera feel mediocre overall.

The phone arrives as a gray‑import built for India, so there is no manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh; you rely on the shop’s limited warranty and pay for any repairs. If you can tolerate the camera compromises and the warranty gap, the device feels like a high‑performance, bright‑screen phone at a lower price.
