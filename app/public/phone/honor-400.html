Is the Honor 400 worth buying?

What is good

  • 6.55 inch 120Hz AMOLED with 5000 nits peak
  • 6000 mAh battery and 80W fast charge
  • 200MP main camera with optical image stabilization
  • Premium matte glass build feels solid

What is not

  • Notification icons disappear after lock screen
  • No eSIM support
  • Overheating during gaming sessions
  • Call drops after about one hour

Buy it if Buy it if you value a bright outdoor display, large battery and high resolution photos.

Skip it if Skip it if you need flawless call quality, smooth heavy gaming or a stable software experience.

Why we say that

Its 6.55 inch AMOLED panel reaches a peak brightness of 5000 nits, so outdoor use is clear. The 6000 mAh battery and 80W charger give a full day of power for many, yet some owners notice faster drain and limited endurance. Performance feels smooth for everyday tasks thanks to the Snapdragon 7 Gen 3 and 12 GB RAM, but gaming can cause the device to heat up and frame rates feel average.

The 200MP main camera with OIS produces sharp images, though the absence of a telephoto lens limits reach and low light results are decent but not flagship level. Build quality feels premium with a matte glass finish, but the software suffers from notification bugs and there is no eSIM support. Call quality may drop after an hour of use.

How is the battery life in daily use?

The 6000 mAh cell can last a full day for many users, especially with moderate use, but several owners notice faster drain and lower endurance in heavy tasks. Expect mixed experiences and keep an eye on charging habits.

Will this phone handle mobile games without problems?

Daily apps run smoothly and the Snapdragon 7 Gen 3 offers decent performance, but gamers report occasional overheating and only average frame rates in demanding titles. For casual play it is fine, heavy gaming may feel limited.

How does the camera perform in low light?

The 200MP main sensor with optical image stabilization captures decent low light shots, though the lack of a telephoto lens limits versatility. Colors remain accurate and detail is good, but night mode is not as strong as flagship rivals.

What is the warranty situation and software update promise?

The cheapest listed price with official warranty is 54,999 taka for the 12 GB 512 GB model. Honor typically provides three to four Android version upgrades on its flagships, and this device is expected to follow a similar schedule.
