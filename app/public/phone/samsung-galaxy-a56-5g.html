Is the Samsung Galaxy A56 5G worth buying?

What is good

  • 1900 nits bright Super AMOLED display
  • Premium aluminum frame with IP67 protection
  • 50 MP main camera with OIS for video
  • 5000 mAh battery capacity

What is not

  • One UI 8.5 update causes lag and heating
  • Charging feels slower than advertised
  • Low light stills are underwhelming
  • Price higher than newer A57 5G

Buy it if Buy if you want a large, bright 5G screen, solid build and can tolerate occasional software hiccups.

Skip it if Skip if you need top tier camera performance, fast charging or a lower price than the newer A57.

Why we say that

The 6.7 inch Super AMOLED panel reaches 1900 nits and runs at 120Hz, making everyday use and media consumption pleasant. The aluminum frame with IP67 rating gives a premium feel and protects against water and dust. The 50MP main sensor with OIS records good video and selfies, while the 5000mAh battery offers decent endurance.

However, the Exynos 1580 chip shows occasional lag and heating under the latest UI, and charging does not feel as fast as the 45W spec suggests. Owners also note that still photos in low light are disappointing and the launch price is steep compared to the newer A57 5G.

How long does the battery last on the Galaxy A56 5G?

The phone packs a 5000 mAh battery that many owners find sufficient for a day of mixed use. Recent software updates have been reported to increase drain, so actual endurance may vary.

Can the Galaxy A56 5G handle gaming smoothly?

The Exynos 1580 processor with 8 GB RAM can run most games at acceptable frame rates, but the One UI 8.5 update has introduced lag and heating that can affect longer sessions.

Is the camera good for low‑light photos?

The 50 MP sensor with OIS records clear video and decent selfies, but owners say still images in low light are underwhelming and lack detail compared to brighter conditions.

Will the phone receive software updates for a long time?

It ships with Android 15 and promises up to six major Android upgrades. Samsung typically provides four to six years of updates for A‑series devices, though the recent One UI 8.5 update has been buggy.
