Is the HTC Wildfire E7 Life worth buying?

What is good

  • Large 5000 mAh battery
  • 6 GB RAM for basic multitasking
  • Clean Android 16 experience
  • Attractive textured back

What is not

  • 10 W charging takes over two hours
  • Low‑resolution dim HD+ display with huge bezels
  • Main camera lacks sharpness and selfies are poor
  • Fingerprint scanner is slow

Buy it if Buy it if you want an inexpensive phone with a big battery, decent RAM and a clean Android experience and can tolerate slow charging and modest performance.

Skip it if Skip it if you need fast charging, a high‑quality camera, or smooth gaming performance.

Why we say that

The 6.74‑inch screen shows a low‑resolution HD+ panel that is dim at about 480 nits and is surrounded by large bezels, so the display feels outdated. The Unisoc T615 chipset with 6 GB RAM handles everyday tasks, but long gaming sessions become laggy and the fingerprint scanner is noticeably slow. The 50 MP rear camera can capture acceptable photos in bright light, yet sharpness suffers and selfies from the 8 MP front lens are poor.

Battery capacity is a strong point at 5000 mAh, yet charging uses an ancient 10 W charger and needs more than two hours to fill. Software runs a clean Android 16 without ads, which many users appreciate. Overall the phone feels like a budget device with a big battery and clean OS, but its slow charging, weak display and camera quality limit its appeal.
