Is the HTC Wildfire E5 Elite worth buying?

What is good

  • 7,000 mAh battery lasts a full day
  • 50 MP main camera works in daylight
  • Clean near‑stock Android without bloatware
  • 6 GB RAM handles everyday apps

What is not

  • 10 W charging is very slow
  • Front looks cheap with big bezels
  • Indoor photos and selfies are weak
  • Free Fire lags after a while

Buy it if Buy it if you need long battery life, a clean Android UI and can accept slow charging and modest camera performance.

Skip it if Skip it if you want fast charging, a premium display or strong gaming performance.

Why we say that

The HTC Wildfire E5 Elite lives up to its low price with a massive 7,000 mAh battery that can easily stretch through a full day of browsing, messaging and video streaming. The 6 GB RAM and Helio G81 chipset keep everyday apps smooth, and the near‑stock Android interface stays free of bloatware. In bright light the 50 MP main sensor produces acceptable photos, but indoor shots and selfies are weak and often noisy.

The 7.2‑inch IPS panel runs at 120 Hz, yet the thick bezels and notch make the display feel cheap, and the 10 W charging (despite an 18 W spec) fills the battery very slowly. Gaming is passable for short sessions but Free Fire stutters after a while. Overall the phone feels overpriced for the hardware, and most owners complain about the slow charge and sub‑par display aesthetics.

The 7,000 mAh cell can keep the phone running for most of a day with normal use such as browsing, messaging and video streaming, but you will need to recharge it overnight because the charging speed is very slow.

Can this phone run games like Free Fire smoothly?

The Helio G81 and 6 GB RAM manage basic tasks, but during longer Free Fire sessions you will notice noticeable lag and frame drops, making the experience less smooth than on higher‑end devices.
