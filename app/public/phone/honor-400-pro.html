Is the Honor 400 Pro worth buying?

What is good

  • 5000 nit peak AMOLED display
  • 200 MP main camera with OIS
  • 100 W fast charging
  • 6000 mAh battery lasting up to two days

What is not

  • Plastic frame despite metal claim
  • USB 2.0 slows file transfers
  • Video jitter and occasional throttling
  • Curved screen makes protectors hard to find

Buy it if If you want a bright, high resolution phone with fast charging and can tolerate a plastic body and occasional performance dips.

Skip it if If you need a metal build, smooth gaming performance, or a reliable low light camera without video jitter.

Why we say that

The Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 chipset and 12 GB RAM deliver flagship speed, yet it can throttle to about 62 percent under sustained stress, leading to occasional frame drops in demanding games. Battery life is a strong point with 8 to 9 hours of screen on time and up to two days of mixed use, and the 100 W charger fills the 6000 mAh cell in under an hour. The display is extremely bright but lacks LTPO and its curved edges make screen protectors hard to find.

Build quality feels solid with IP68/69 rating, but the frame is plastic despite metal claims. Owners love the fast charging and high resolution camera, but complain about USB 2.0 data speed, video jitter, and the limited regional update support.

How long does the battery last in everyday use?

Owners report about 8 to 9 hours of screen on time and up to two days of mixed use, so the 6000 mAh cell gives elite endurance for most daily routines.

Will the phone receive Android updates for a long period?

Honor promises up to six major Android upgrades and the device ships with Android 15, but only the EU region currently gets the full update schedule, so other regions may see fewer updates.

Is the camera reliable in low light and video recording?

The 200 MP main sensor with OIS and a 3x tele lens captures sharp portraits in good light, but owners notice soft selfies and some jitter in video, and low light performance is not highlighted.
