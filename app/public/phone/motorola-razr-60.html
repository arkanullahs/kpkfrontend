Is the Motorola Razr 60 worth buying?

What is good

  • Bright foldable display with 3000 nits
  • 50MP main camera with optical image stabilization
  • Near stock Android experience
  • 5G connectivity and ample storage options

What is not

  • Battery life below average
  • Performance lags behind cheaper phones
  • Camera improvements stalled since last year
  • UFS 2.2 storage feels slow

Buy it if Buy it if you want a premium foldable with a vivid screen and decent camera and can accept modest performance and battery life.

Skip it if Skip it if you need strong gaming performance, long battery endurance, or a fast storage solution.

Why we say that

The 6.9 inch LTPO AMOLED panel reaches 3000 nits and runs at 120Hz, making everyday use feel smooth and vivid. The 50MP main sensor with OIS captures decent photos in good light, and the 32MP selfie camera is adequate for video calls. Android 15 with a near stock UI feels clean and updates are limited to a few versions, so future software support may lag.

The Dimensity 7400X chipset paired with 8 to 12GB RAM handles basic apps well but falls behind cheaper phones in gaming and heavy multitasking. Battery life is below average for a 4500mAh cell, and owners report it does not last a full day under moderate use. The device arrives as a gray‑import unit, so you only get a shop warranty and must verify IMEI and BTRC status before buying.
