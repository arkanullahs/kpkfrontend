Is the Motorola Edge 60 Pro worth buying?

What is good

  • 6.7 inch 120Hz OLED with 4500‑nit peak brightness
  • 6000 mAh battery with 90W fast charging
  • 50 MP main camera with OIS and 3× telephoto
  • Android 15 with near‑stock Hello UI

What is not

  • Thermal throttling during sustained gaming
  • Video stabilization jitter and no 4K 60fps
  • No manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh

Buy it if Buy it if you want a large, bright screen, long battery life and a strong still‑photo camera and are okay with shop‑only warranty.

Skip it if Skip it if you need reliable 4K 60fps video, heavy gaming performance or an official brand warranty.

Why we say that

It sports a 6.7‑inch quad‑curved P‑OLED panel with 120 Hz refresh and a peak brightness of 4500 nits, making outdoor use easy. The 6000 mAh battery paired with 90 W fast charging lives up to the high battery rating, and most owners report a full day of heavy use without a top‑up. Its 50 MP main sensor with OIS and a 3× telephoto lens produces strong stills, while the 50 MP selfie camera is also sharp.

Video, however, suffers from jittery stabilization and the device does not record 4K at 60 fps, which many reviewers find disappointing. Performance is adequate for daily tasks thanks to the Dimensity 8350 Extreme chipset and 8 GB RAM, but owners notice thermal throttling during extended gaming sessions, lowering frame rates. The phone runs Android 15 with Motorola’s near‑stock Hello UI, offering up to three major OS upgrades, though occasional UI jitters and some bloatware have been reported.

Because every unit is a gray‑import built for India, there is no official manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh; you must rely on the shop’s limited warranty and pay for any repairs.
