Is the Samsung Galaxy A35 5G worth buying?

What is good

  • 6.6" Super AMOLED with 120 Hz and 1000 nits brightness
  • 8 GB RAM and up to 256 GB storage
  • IP67 rating with Gorilla Glass Victus+
  • 50 MP main sensor with OIS

What is not

  • 25 W charging is slow and no adapter included
  • Display flickers at low brightness and has large bezels
  • Heats up during mobile data use and gaming

Buy it if Buy it if you value a bright, water‑resistant screen, decent camera and official Samsung support, and you can live with occasional heating and slower gaming.

Skip it if Skip it if you need top‑tier gaming, low‑light photography or the cheapest 5G option.

Why we say that

The 6.6" Super AMOLED panel reaches 1000 nits and runs at 120 Hz, making everyday use pleasant. The Exynos 1380 with 8 GB RAM handles apps and Android 14 smoothly, yet it struggles when you push it for long gaming sessions. Battery capacity is 5000 mAh, but standby drain and data‑heavy usage reduce endurance and raise temperature.

Charging at 25 W is noticeably slow and the box does not include an adapter. The glass build is premium with IP67 protection, though the bezels are large and the display can flicker at low brightness. The 50 MP main camera with OIS captures clear photos in good light, but low‑light and video quality are disappointing.

Overall it feels like a mid‑range device priced higher than the newer A53 5G, so weigh the strengths against the heating and performance limits before buying.

The 5000 mAh battery gives solid capacity, but standby drain and mobile data usage reduce endurance. Heavy data or network aggregation can cause noticeable heating, so you may need to charge more often than expected.

Does the camera work well in low light?

The 50 MP main sensor with OIS produces consistent photos in good lighting, yet it struggles in low‑light situations and video recording, delivering less detail and more noise than expected.
