Is the Samsung Galaxy A17 5G worth buying?

What is good

  • 50MP main camera with OIS
  • 6.7 inch Super AMOLED display
  • 5000 mAh battery lasts a day
  • Six years of Android updates

What is not

  • Frequent system stutters
  • 25W charging feels slow
  • Plastic frame and thick bezels
  • Waterdrop notch looks dated

Buy it if Buy if you want a large AMOLED screen, solid main camera and long software support at a mid‑range price.

Skip it if Skip if you need smooth gaming, fast charging or a premium build.

Why we say that

The 6.7 inch Super AMOLED panel delivers vivid colors and 800‑nit peak brightness, while the 50MP main sensor with OIS captures decent photos in good light. The 5000 mAh battery easily lasts a full day of typical use, but the 25W charger feels slow compared with newer phones. Everyday tasks often suffer from UI lag and occasional stutters, making multitasking feel less fluid.

The plastic frame, thick bezels and dated water‑drop notch give the phone a cheap feel despite its IP54 rating. Gaming is limited to casual titles because the Exynos 1330 struggles with heavier games. At around 30,999 taka for the official warranty model, it competes closely with the newer Galaxy A26 5G, which many consider a better overall purchase.

GSMArena finds the Galaxy A17 5G tough to recommend at its current asking price. While they appreciate the inclusion of Gorilla Glass Victus, optical image stabilization on the main camera, and Samsung's promise of long software support, the phone's sluggish performance and lack of meaningful upgrades over the previous generation hold it back. They suggest waiting for a price drop or looking at last year's alternatives.

SamZone concludes that the A17 5G is a mixed bag, offering great daytime camera performance and excellent software longevity, but severely dropping the ball on modern design. He heavily criticizes the outdated waterdrop notch and the somewhat choppy user experience caused by the Exynos chipset, noting that while it is an okay choice for loyal Samsung fans, the current price tag does not fully justify the compromises.

Tech Shibly considers the phone a potentially great value, primarily praising its bright AMOLED display, stable gaming temperatures, and impressive battery optimization. However, he also agrees that the waterdrop notch and the absence of 4K video recording are noticeable drawbacks. He believes that if the price falls closer to the 20,000 Taka mark, it will be a fantastic deal for anyone seeking a reliable, long-lasting device.
