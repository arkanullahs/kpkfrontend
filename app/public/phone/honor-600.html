Is the Honor 600 worth buying?

What is good

  • Industry‑leading 7000 mAh battery life
  • 200 MP main sensor with OIS
  • 6.57‑inch AMOLED with 8000 nits peak brightness
  • 80 W fast charging

What is not

  • Software updates are irregular
  • USB‑C 2.0 transfer only about 45 MB/s
  • Gaming performance modest for price

Buy it if Buy it if you value massive battery life, high‑resolution photos and can accept occasional software quirks.

Skip it if Skip it if you expect top‑tier gaming performance or guaranteed long‑term software support.

Why we say that

The 7000 mAh battery truly lasts beyond a typical workday even with video streaming and gaming. The 200 MP main sensor captures detailed photos and the 6.57‑inch AMOLED screen reaches an eye‑catching 8000 nits peak brightness, making outdoor use easy. 80 W charging refills the large battery quickly, and the IR blaster adds a handy remote function.

However, owners complain that the Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 feels modest for the price, especially in demanding games, and the software update schedule is irregular, with some regions receiving updates later than others. Data transfer over the USB‑C 2.0 port is slow, averaging around 45 MB per second. The lack of a telephoto lens also limits zoom capabilities.

Overall, the phone excels in endurance and imaging but falls short on performance consistency and long‑term software support.
