Why we say that

Its Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset keeps frame rates steady at 144 fps and the 6.82 inch LTPO AMOLED screen stays bright even in direct sun. The 6150 mAh battery easily lasts a full day of mixed use and the 120W charger refills it in under half an hour. Owners praise the premium build, IP68/69 protection and the fast ultrasonic fingerprint sensor.

The main camera is decent in daylight but struggles in low light and the new 2x telephoto lens feels like a step back from the previous 3x periscope. Software feels fluid but is packed with bloatware and security patches arrive slowly, especially on the China‑origin OriginOS version. Because every unit is a gray import, there is no manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh; you rely on the shop’s limited cover and must pay for any repairs.

How long does the battery last on a typical day?

The iQOO 13 houses a 6150 mAh cell that most owners say easily covers a full day of mixed usage, and the 120W charger can refill it in less than thirty minutes, giving you plenty of buffer.

Is the iQOO 13 good for heavy gaming?

Yes, the Snapdragon 8 Elite processor together with 12 GB RAM keeps games running at a stable 144 fps and the phone’s thermal system stays cool, so you rarely see throttling even in long sessions.

How does the camera perform in low‑light conditions?

The 50 MP main sensor delivers decent pictures in daylight, but owners report that low‑light shots are noisy and lack detail, and the 2x telephoto lens does not help much in dim environments.

All iQOO 13 units in Bangladesh are gray imports, so there is no manufacturer warranty; you rely on the shop’s limited cover and must pay for repairs. Software receives up to three major Android upgrades but updates arrive slowly, especially on the China‑origin OriginOS version.
