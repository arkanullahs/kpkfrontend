Is the Motorola Razr 40 Ultra worth buying?

What is good

  • 165 Hz LTPO AMOLED display
  • Durable foldable chassis
  • Snapdragon 8+ Gen 1 performance
  • Near‑stock Android experience

What is not

  • Battery life modest and charging slow
  • Frequent hardware failures like charging IC
  • Software updates likely ending soon
  • Gray‑import only, no official warranty

Buy it if Buy if you prioritize a premium foldable screen and can accept limited warranty and possible hardware issues.

Skip it if Skip if you need reliable battery life, long‑term software support, or an official brand warranty.

Why we say that

Owners love the 165 Hz LTPO AMOLED screen and the smooth Snapdragon 8+ Gen 1 performance, yet they complain about modest battery life and slow 30 W charging. The 3800 mAh cell often feels short on a full day and charging can be unreliable, with reports of burnt charging ICs and faulty capacitors that shut the phone off. Build quality is generally good and the device feels durable, but being a gray‑import means there is no official Motorola warranty and you must rely on the shop’s warranty after confirming IMEI and BTRC status.

Software updates have already slowed, the last Android version is 15 and security patches end in June 2027, so long‑term support is limited. Overall it feels like a premium screen with a budget‑grade battery and uncertain after‑sales support.
