Realme C100x Is it worth buying?

The Realme C100x is a heavy, bulky device that struggles with basic performance due to its outdated Unisoc T7250 chipset and slow eMMC 5.1 storage.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You need a very sturdy, heavy-duty phone with a large battery and do not mind slow performance or an outdated design.
Consider alternatives if
You play games, use many apps, or expect a smooth, modern software experience without ads.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

Review sources ↓

What works well4

  • Massive 8000 mAh battery capacity
  • Sturdy and drop-resistant build
  • IP64 rated water and dust resistance
  • Bright 900 nits peak display

Reported trade-offs4

  • Severe performance lag and slow loading
  • Outdated waterdrop notch design
  • Poor 720p selfie video quality
  • Heavy bloatware and ads in software

Why we say that

While the 8000 mAh battery is massive, the 12nm chipset efficiency is poor, meaning you do not get the full benefit of that capacity. Owners report severe lag during daily tasks and gaming, with apps taking a long time to load. The design feels dated with a waterdrop notch, and the software experience is cluttered with heavy bloatware and ads.

Camera performance is mediocre, and the 720p selfie video quality is widely considered unacceptable for a phone released in 2026. If you prioritize speed or a modern user experience, this device will likely frustrate you.

Is the Realme C100x good for gaming?

No, the phone is not suitable for gaming. Owners report severe lag and slow loading times caused by the Unisoc T7250 chipset and eMMC 5.1 storage. Gaming performance is rated very low by users.

How long will the Realme C100x receive software updates?

The device runs on Android 16 and uses Realme UI. It is scheduled to receive 2 to 3 OS updates over its lifespan. Be aware that the software experience includes a medium to high amount of bloatware and ads.

The phone features a massive 8000 mAh battery which users rate highly. However, the efficiency is hampered by the 12nm chipset, which consumes more power than expected during daily use.
