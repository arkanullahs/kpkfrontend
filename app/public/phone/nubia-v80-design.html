Nubia V80 Design Is it worth buying?

Beware that the Nubia V80 Design looks like a cheap iPhone 17 Pro copy but delivers only modest performance.

Why we say that

The 6.75 inch IPS LCD can reach 1000 nits, which makes outdoor use easy. The 5000 mAh battery and 22W charger give you roughly a day of normal use, but the bright screen can drain it faster. Storage is generous with 256 GB and 8 GB RAM, yet the Unisoc T7280 SoC struggles with games and heavy apps.

The 50 MP main camera is advertised, but owners say only one lens is real and low‑light shots are disappointing. Software runs Nebula AIOS on Android 16, but updates are limited to one or two versions and many users complain about ads and bloat. Official warranty is available through the brand’s service network, but the price has jumped from around 13‑14 k to nearly 21 k taka.

How long does the battery last in everyday use?

The phone has a 5000 mAh battery and a 22W charger. With moderate daily tasks the battery can last about a full day, but bright screen usage and heavy apps may require charging by evening.

Is the Nubia V80 Design suitable for gaming?

Gaming performance is weak because the device uses the Unisoc T7280 chipset. Owner ratings show low scores for gaming, so it can handle casual games but will struggle with demanding titles.

Does the camera work well in low‑light conditions?

The phone advertises a 50 MP main camera with f/1.8 aperture, but owners report fake camera modules and poor low‑light results. Night photos are generally noisy and lack detail.

Will the phone receive regular Android updates?

It runs Nebula AIOS based on Android 16 and has received only one or two OS updates so far. Users complain about poor update support, so future updates are likely limited.
