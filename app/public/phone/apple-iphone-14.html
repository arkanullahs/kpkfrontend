Is the Apple iPhone 14 worth buying?

What is good

  • A15 Bionic delivers smooth daily and gaming performance
  • Super Retina XDR OLED shows vivid colors
  • iOS receives clean updates for many years
  • Resale value remains strong in Bangladesh

What is not

  • 60 Hz screen feels choppy compared to 120 Hz Androids
  • Lightning port and slow charging are outdated
  • Battery capacity small and health drops quickly
  • Frame paint may peel without a case

Buy it if Buy it if you value iOS stability, strong resale value, and solid camera performance.

Skip it if Skip it if you need a high‑refresh display, fast charging, or long‑lasting battery.

Why we say that

The A15 Bionic runs daily apps and games without hiccups, and the Super Retina XDR OLED delivers vivid colors. iOS remains clean and receives updates for many years, which many owners appreciate. However, the 60 Hz screen feels noticeably choppy compared to newer 120 Hz Android phones, and the Lightning port with slow charging feels outdated.

Battery capacity is modest and health can drop quickly, so heavy users may need to charge more often. The premium build feels solid and is IP68 rated, yet the frame paint can peel without a case. Camera quality is natural with good selfies, but the missing telephoto lens limits zoom quality.

Overall, it is a reliable device for iOS fans who value resale value, but it shows its age in several key areas.

Is the battery life good for everyday use?

Can I play games smoothly on this phone?

The A15 Bionic still runs games smoothly and thermal improvements keep throttling low during long sessions. Users rate gaming performance eight out of ten, so casual and demanding titles work well.

How is the camera performance in low light?

The 12 MP main sensor with f/1.5 aperture and OIS captures natural colors in low light, but the lack of a telephoto lens means zoomed shots lose quality. Portraits are not as sharp.
