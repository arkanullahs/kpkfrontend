Is the Motorola G67 worth buying?

What is good

  • High-quality 120Hz AMOLED display
  • Clean near-stock Hello UI
  • Reliable Android 16 software experience

What is not

  • Outdated UFS 2.2 storage and A76 cores
  • Poor gaming and camera performance
  • Overpriced compared to newer models
  • Insufficient 4GB RAM options

Buy it if You prioritize a clean software interface and a high-quality display above all other hardware specifications.

Skip it if You are a gamer or a power user who requires fast storage and reliable performance for your daily tasks.

Why we say that

While the 6.78-inch AMOLED display is a high-quality upgrade, the rest of the hardware struggles to keep pace with 2026 standards. Owners report significant frustration with the Dimensity 6300 chipset, specifically citing outdated A76 cores and slow UFS 2.2 storage as major bottlenecks. Gaming performance is particularly poor, and the battery life is considered a downgrade compared to previous Power models.

Although the clean Hello UI and Android 16 software experience are praised, the hardware limitations make it difficult to recommend at 26,990 taka. Most buyers find the newer Motorola G100 to be a better value at 26,490 taka. Since this is only available as unofficial gray-import stock, you will rely on a shop warranty rather than a brand warranty, making it essential to verify the IMEI and BTRC status before purchase.
