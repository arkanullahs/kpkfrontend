Is the iQOO 15R worth buying?

What is good

  • Snapdragon 8 Gen 5 performance
  • 7600 mAh battery with 100 W fast charge
  • 5000 nit peak brightness on AMOLED
  • 6.59‑inch 144 Hz display

What is not

  • USB 2.0 port limits data speed
  • No LTPO variable refresh
  • No eSIM support
  • Camera tuning feels mediocre

Buy it if Buy it if you want flagship speed and marathon gaming on a budget.

Skip it if Skip it if you need reliable camera performance or official brand warranty.

Why we say that

The phone feels fast in games and everyday apps, and the 7600 mAh cell lives through heavy use without a charge. The 6.59‑inch AMOLED panel shines brightly with a 5000‑nit peak and smooth 144 Hz refresh, yet it lacks LTPO, so the refresh rate stays fixed. Camera hardware is solid on paper, but owners complain about bland tuning and the missing telephoto lens.

Software runs Android 16 with OriginOS 6, but iManager cannot be removed and there is medium bloat. The gray‑import version only carries a shop warranty, so you must confirm IMEI and BTRC status. USB ports are limited to USB 2.0, and the device does not support eSIM, which some users find disappointing.

How long will the iQOO 15R receive software updates?

The phone launches with Android 16 and OriginOS 6 and promises up to four major Android upgrades. The global Funtouch version receives three OS updates, so you can expect several years of software support.

Is the battery good for day‑long heavy use?

The 7600 mAh battery earned a 9.7 owner rating and reviewers call its endurance insane. It easily lasts beyond a full day even with gaming, and the 100 W charger refills it very quickly.

Will the camera perform well in low light?

The main camera uses a 50 MP 1/1.56" sensor with f/1.8 aperture and OIS, but owners say the tuning is mediocre and there is no telephoto lens, so low‑light photos are acceptable but not exceptional.
