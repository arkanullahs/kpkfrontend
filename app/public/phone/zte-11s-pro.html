Buying verdict

ZTE 11S Pro Is it worth buying?

If you want a phone that can game for hours without charging, the ZTE 11S Pro delivers.

Choose it if
You need a gaming phone with massive battery and can accept gray‑import warranty limits.
Consider alternatives if
You prioritize camera performance or official after‑sales support.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

What works well

  • 7500 mAh battery lasts all day
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 gives top performance
  • 80 W fast charging restores power quickly
  • 144 Hz AMOLED display looks vivid

Reported trade-offs

  • Camera quality is weak for price
  • Heavy and uncomfortable to hold
  • No official warranty in Bangladesh
  • Peak brightness may cause issues

Why we say that

The 7500 mAh battery keeps the device alive even under heavy gaming loads and the 80 W charger refills it quickly. The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset together with 12 GB RAM provides smooth performance and the 144 Hz AMOLED screen looks vivid, though the 1800‑nit peak brightness can be a red flag in bright sunlight. The active liquid cooling and 24k RPM fan keep temperatures low, so long gaming sessions stay stable.

Build quality feels premium but the large size and weight make it uncomfortable to hold for extended periods. The camera system is disappointing for a phone in this price range, lacking telephoto and producing low‑light video that looks dated. The device arrives as a gray import, so there is no official ZTE warranty in Bangladesh and any repair will depend on the shop’s policy.

How long does the battery last during heavy use?

The 7500 mAh cell provides exceptional endurance even under heavy load, so you can expect a full day of gaming or media use before needing to recharge.

Is the phone good for gaming?

Yes, the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset, 12 GB RAM, active liquid cooling and 24k RPM fan keep frame rates high and prevent throttling, allowing smooth play of demanding titles like GTA 5.

Will the camera work well in low light?

The 50 MP main sensor lacks telephoto and uses basic OmniVision tuning, resulting in poor low‑light photos and video that many owners compare to an old Blackberry.

What warranty and service can I expect in Bangladesh?

All listings are gray imports, so there is no manufacturer warranty. Repairs are paid and depend on the shop’s own warranty policy, and ZTE has little official service presence in the country.
