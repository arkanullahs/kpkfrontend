Buying verdict

Apple iPhone 15 Plus Is it worth buying?

Overall the iPhone 15 Plus feels like a premium device that still shows its 2023 roots.

Choose it if
You want iOS performance, solid battery life and a premium build at a lower price than the newer iPhone 16 Plus.
Consider alternatives if
You need a high‑refresh display, fast charging, or an official Apple warranty in Bangladesh.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

Review sources ↓

What works well

  • A16 Bionic delivers strong overall performance
  • 48 MP main camera captures detailed photos
  • Battery often outlasts the Pro Max
  • Premium frosted back with IP68 protection

Reported trade-offs

  • 60 Hz display feels laggy compared to newer screens
  • 20 W charging is slow and can heat
  • Ultrawide camera produces soft, low‑detail images
  • Overheating may cause throttling during heavy use

Why we say that

The A16 Bionic gives smooth performance in everyday tasks and most games, and the 4383 mAh battery often outlasts the larger Pro Max. The 48 MP main camera produces sharp photos and the frosted back with IP68 feels solid. However the 6.7‑inch OLED panel is stuck at 60 Hz, which many owners describe as laggy compared to newer 120 Hz screens.

Charging at 20 W is noticeably slow and can generate heat, and the ultrawide lens is soft and lacks detail. Overheating during heavy gaming or charging is reported and can trigger throttling. Being a gray‑import, there is no Apple warranty in Bangladesh; repairs must be paid to the shop.

How long does the battery last in everyday use?

Owners rate the battery at 9.3 out of 10 and report that the 4383 mAh cell often lasts longer than the iPhone 15 Pro Max, easily covering a full day of mixed use without needing a charge.

Can the iPhone 15 Plus run modern games smoothly?

The A16 Bionic keeps games like Call of Duty stable at 60 fps and owners give it a 7.8 gaming rating, but the 60 Hz screen and occasional overheating can limit peak performance.

How good is the camera, especially the ultrawide lens, in low light?

The 48 MP main sensor with f/1.6 aperture and OIS delivers sharp images and a useful 2× crop, but reviewers note the ultrawide lens is soft and lacks fine detail, especially in dim conditions.
