Honor Magic V6 Is it worth buying?

If you can tolerate a few software quirks and need a massive battery, the Honor Magic V6 feels like a premium foldable at a gray import price.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You want a large foldable screen, strong camera and long battery and are okay with handling occasional software and connectivity hiccups.
Consider alternatives if
You need a phone with official warranty support or cannot tolerate thermal throttling during intensive tasks.

Why we say that

The 7.95 inch foldable AMOLED reaches a 5000 nit peak, making outdoor use easy. The 6660 mAh silicon carbon battery lasts well beyond a full day even with heavy use. The 50 MP main sensor delivers sharp stills and decent zoom, though video stabilization can wobble.

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 offers strong performance, but the CPU cuts power in half unless you enable performance mode, and the phone can get hot during long gaming sessions. Wi Fi and Bluetooth connections are sometimes unstable, and notifications may be hit or miss. The build is IP69 rated and thin, but there is no official manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh; only the shop’s limited cover applies.

How long will the battery last with regular use?

The 6660 mAh silicon carbon battery is rated for industry leading endurance. Most owners report it easily covers a full day of mixed usage, and it can stretch to two days with light use.

Can the phone handle heavy gaming without overheating?

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 provides strong gaming performance, but the CPU reduces speed unless you switch to performance mode. Expect some heat and reduced frame rates if you play for long periods without that mode.

Is the camera reliable in low light conditions?

The 50 MP sensor with a f/1.6 aperture captures good stills in low light, but video can show occasional jitter. Overall the camera is solid for night photography, especially for still images.

All listings are gray import units, so there is no official manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh. The shop may offer its own limited cover, and any repairs will be paid out of pocket.
