Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra Is it worth buying?

If you can afford a premium foldable and accept a gray‑import warranty, the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra delivers a stunning screen and flagship performance.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You want a top‑tier foldable with flagship speed and can handle a high price and shop‑only warranty.
Consider alternatives if
You need reliable battery life, an official Samsung warranty, or a compact phone.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

Review sources ↓

What works well4

  • 8 inch 3000 nit foldable display
  • 200 MP main camera with OIS
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 performance
  • Thin premium build quality

Reported trade-offs4

  • Thermal throttling under heavy load
  • Battery life short for foldable size
  • No periscope zoom, only 3x tele
  • Keyboard occupies most of inner screen

Why we say that

The 8 inch Dynamic LTPO AMOLED 2X panel reaches 3000 nits and shows almost no crease, making everyday use a visual treat. The 200 MP main sensor captures detailed photos and the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset scores high in benchmarks and gaming, though owners notice the device heats up and may throttle under long sessions. Battery life is modest for a 5000 mAh cell, and the silicon‑carbon tech does not fully offset the power draw of the large display.

The lack of a periscope telephoto lens limits zoom flexibility, and the on‑screen keyboard can dominate the inner display. Price is at the top end of the market and there is no official Samsung warranty, only the shop’s promise, so repairs are paid out of pocket.

Will the battery last a full day with normal use?

The phone has a 5000 mAh battery and uses silicon‑carbon technology, but owners rate battery life at 6.8 out of 10. Expect a full day of light use, but heavy screen time will likely require a midday charge.

How does it handle gaming and heavy apps?

Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 provides strong performance and gaming scores around 8.2, yet many users report heating and occasional throttling during long sessions, which can lower frame rates after a while.

Is the camera good in low‑light situations?

The 200 MP sensor with f/1.7 aperture and OIS captures detailed images, and owners give the camera a 7.5 rating. However, the lack of a periscope zoom means low‑light zoom shots are limited.
