Is the Realme Note 60 worth buying?

What is good

  • Durable die‑cast aluminum body
  • 5,000 mAh battery gives full‑day endurance
  • 90 Hz IPS panel for smoother scrolling
  • 32 MP main camera delivers acceptable photos

What is not

  • 10 W charging is painfully slow
  • Outdated T612 chipset struggles with games
  • Screen visibility drops in direct sunlight
  • Software updates introduce noticeable lag

Buy it if Buy it if you value durability, decent camera and long battery life over fast charging, high‑end gaming, and bright outdoor display.

Skip it if Skip it if you prioritize fast charging, smooth multitasking, or clear visibility in direct sunlight.

Why we say that

Its die‑cast aluminum chassis feels solid and survives everyday bumps, while the 5,000 mAh battery easily gets you through a typical day's use. The 90 Hz IPS screen makes scrolling feel smoother, and the 32 MP rear camera produces acceptable shots in good light, with natural‑looking selfies. However, owners constantly mention three pain points: the 10 W charger refills the battery very slowly, the outdated Unisoc Tiger T612 processor struggles with multitasking and heavy games, and the screen becomes hard to read under direct sunlight.

Software updates bring the latest Android version but also add medium‑high ads and noticeable lag, making the overall experience feel a bit sluggish after each update. Consider whether these compromises fit your daily routine before purchasing.

How long does the battery last on a typical day?

The 5,000 mAh battery typically lasts a full day of moderate use, and owners rate its endurance as good. The downside is the 10 W charger, which takes many hours to fill the battery.

Can the Realme Note 60 handle modern mobile games?

The phone runs on a Unisoc Tiger T612 chipset, which is considered outdated. It can manage basic apps, but multitasking and heavy games feel laggy, reflected in a low gaming rating of 3 out of 10.

Is the camera good in low‑light situations?

A 32 MP main sensor with an f/1.8 aperture provides decent photos in good light and selfies look natural. In low‑light conditions the results are acceptable for a budget phone but not comparable to higher‑end models.

Will the phone receive software updates and how stable are they?

Realme ships Android 14 with Realme UI Lite and promises about two to three major OS updates. The UI adds medium‑high ads and after each update users report noticeable lag, so stability may decline over time.
