Buying verdict

Apple iPhone 17 Is it worth buying?

If you want a phone with a top tier display and fast performance, the iPhone 17 delivers that but you must accept a modest battery and heating issues.

Choose it if
You value a smooth high refresh screen, strong camera and long software support and can handle the higher price for an official warranty.
Consider alternatives if
You need long battery endurance, heavy gaming without throttling, or fast USB data transfer.

What works well

  • 120 Hz LTPO OLED display

Reported trade-offs

  • Battery capacity feels small
  • Device gets very hot under load
  • Charging slows after 80 percent
  • USB C limited to USB 2.0 speeds

Why we say that

The 6.3 inch LTPO Super Retina XDR OLED panel runs at 120 Hz and can peak at 3000 nits, making outdoor use easy. The Apple A19 chip with 8 GB RAM scores 9.4 out of 10 in performance and runs iOS 26, which stays clean and will receive five to six major updates. The 48 MP main camera with optical image stabilization captures sharp photos, though there is no telephoto lens.

Battery capacity is only 3692 mAh, so most users see four to seven hours of screen on time and the phone gets very hot during charging or heavy use, causing thermal throttling. Charging at 40 W is quick at first but slows after 80 percent, and the USB C port is limited to USB 2.0 data speeds. Official warranty starts at ৳1,47,999 and applies only to official units; cheaper gray import listings around ৳1,02,500 have no manufacturer cover.

How long will the battery last in daily use?

The 3692 mAh battery provides roughly four to seven hours of screen on time depending on brightness and apps. Light use may stretch a day, but intensive tasks and the 120 Hz display drain it faster.

Can the iPhone 17 handle gaming without slowing down?

The A19 processor and 8 GB RAM give high performance scores, but the phone heats up quickly under sustained load and throttles, so long gaming sessions may feel slower than on devices with vapor chamber cooling.

Is the camera good in low light situations?

The 48 MP sensor with a large 1/1.56 inch size and f/1.6 aperture captures bright images, and optical image stabilization helps, but the lack of a telephoto lens means zoomed low light shots lose detail.
