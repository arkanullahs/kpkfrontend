Is the Honor X6d worth buying?

What is good

  • 50MP main camera
  • 5260 mAh battery
  • 90Hz display
  • Headphone jack

What is not

  • Only 4 GB RAM limits multitasking
  • 5G not working in some regions
  • Medium ads and bloat in software

Buy it if Buy it if you want a big battery and a solid main camera without spending more than 20,000 taka.

Skip it if Skip it if you need smooth multitasking, reliable 5G everywhere, or a strong selfie camera.

Why we say that

The 6.75‑inch 90Hz TFT LCD is bright enough for indoor use with 450 nits peak, and the 50MP f/1.8 sensor captures clear photos in good light. The 5260 mAh cell lasts most of a day, and the 15W charger refills it without rush. The Dimensity 6300 chipset runs Android 16 with MagicOS 10, but only 4 GB of RAM makes heavy multitasking feel sluggish.

Owners report that 5G drops out in some regions and that the software shows a moderate amount of ads and bloat. The 5MP front camera is noticeably weaker than the rear unit. Overall it feels like a budget flagship that delivers on battery and main camera while falling short on speed, 5G reliability, and software polish.

The 5260 mAh battery can easily last a full day of mixed use such as browsing, social media, and video playback. When it runs low, the 15W charger refills it in about an hour and a half.

Can the Honor X6d run mobile games smoothly?

The Dimensity 6300 chipset handles most casual games well, but the 4 GB RAM limits performance in demanding titles and multitasking. Expect occasional slowdowns during heavy gaming sessions.

Is the 50MP camera good in low‑light conditions?

The 50MP sensor with an f/1.8 aperture captures decent photos in low light, but the overall camera rating of 5.5 shows it is not exceptional. The 5MP selfie camera struggles more in dim environments.
