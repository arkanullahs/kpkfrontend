Is the Vivo Y400 Pro worth buying?

What is good

  • 5500 mAh battery lasts over a day
  • 90 W fast charging
  • Bright 6.77‑inch AMOLED with 120 Hz
  • IP69 and military‑grade build

What is not

  • UFS 2.2 storage slows game loading
  • No ultrawide lens
  • Main camera lacks OIS
  • No official manufacturer warranty

Buy it if Buy it if you need a rugged phone with long battery life and fast charging and can accept slower storage and a limited camera setup.

Skip it if Skip it if you expect top‑tier gaming performance, an official warranty, or a complete camera system with ultrawide and OIS.

Why we say that

The 6.77‑inch curved AMOLED is bright enough for outdoor use and the 120 Hz panel feels smooth. Battery life is a strong point, easily lasting more than a day, and the 90 W charger fills it from zero to half in about twenty minutes. Build quality feels premium with IP69 and military‑grade durability, but the storage is only UFS 2.2, so apps and games load noticeably slower than on newer phones.

The main camera captures detailed photos in good light, yet it lacks optical image stabilization and an ultrawide lens, making low‑light and wide‑angle shots disappointing. Software runs Android 15 with medium bloatware and will receive only a couple of major updates. The biggest practical drawback is the lack of any official manufacturer warranty; you rely on the shop’s limited guarantee and must verify the IMEI and BTRC status before buying.
