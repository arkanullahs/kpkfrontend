POCO M7 Plus Is it worth buying?

If you can live with a heavy, bulky phone and no official warranty, the POCO M7 Plus will keep you running for two days on a single charge.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You need massive battery life, a large fast display, and can accept the weight and lack of brand warranty.
Consider alternatives if

What works well4

  • 2‑day+ battery life
  • Huge 6.9‑inch 144 Hz screen
  • 50 MP daylight camera
  • 5G connectivity and IR blaster

Reported trade-offs4

  • No official warranty, only shop warranty
  • Heavy and bulky, hard one‑hand use
  • Poor low‑light camera performance
  • Muffled speaker and no headphone jack

Why we say that

The 7000 mAh silicon‑carbon battery is the standout feature, easily lasting over 48 hours with moderate use. The 6.9‑inch IPS LCD offers a bright 850‑nit panel and a smooth 144 Hz refresh rate, making media consumption comfortable despite the lack of AMOLED contrast. Performance is adequate for everyday tasks thanks to the Snapdragon 6s Gen 3 and 6 GB RAM, but gaming is limited to low settings and suffers without a gyroscope.

The 50 MP main sensor produces decent daylight photos, yet low‑light shots are noisy and lack detail. Audio is a disappointment with muffled speakers and no 3.5 mm jack. Software runs on HyperOS 2 with Android 15, but ads and bloatware are noticeable.

How long does the battery last on a normal day?

The POCO M7 Plus uses a 7000 mAh silicon‑carbon battery that typically lasts over two days with moderate usage such as browsing, social media, and video playback. Heavy gaming will reduce endurance.

Can I play games smoothly on this phone?

Casual games run smoothly thanks to the Snapdragon 6s Gen 3 and 6 GB RAM, but the device lacks a gyroscope and has no vapor‑chamber cooling, so demanding titles need low graphics settings to stay playable.

Is the camera good in low‑light conditions?

The 50 MP main sensor captures decent photos in daylight, but in low‑light situations the images become noisy and lack detail. Expect poorer results compared to phones with larger sensors or night‑mode optimization.

Will I get an official warranty and how many Android updates will it receive?

All listings are gray‑import, so there is no manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh; you rely on the shop’s limited warranty and must pay for repairs. The phone promises up to two major Android upgrades.
