Redmi Note 12 4G Is it worth buying?

If you want a bright large screen and long battery at a rock‑bottom price, this phone can deliver, but expect compromises.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You need a large vivid display and solid battery at a very low price and can accept a gray‑import with no official warranty or smooth gaming.
Consider alternatives if
You rely on reliable gaming performance, frequent software updates, or official brand support.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

Review sources ↓

What works well4

  • Bright 120Hz AMOLED screen
  • 5000 mAh battery lasts a day
  • 33W fast charging
  • 48 MP camera captures decent photos

Reported trade-offs4

  • No manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh
  • Gaming and multitasking feel laggy
  • Software is heavy with ads
  • Software updates likely ended

Why we say that

The 6.67‑inch AMOLED panel with 120 Hz refresh and 1200‑nit peak brightness is genuinely vivid and smooth for media consumption. The 5000 mAh cell gives a full day of mixed use, and the 33 W charger refills it reasonably fast. The Snapdragon 4 Gen 1 chipset with 4‑8 GB RAM handles everyday apps, yet owners report noticeable lag when switching apps or playing games, and multitasking feels almost impossible.

The 48 MP main sensor produces decent photos in good light, but low‑light performance is average and the camera can feel sluggish. The phone runs MIUI with heavy ads and bloatware, and software updates have largely stopped after four years. It arrives as a gray‑import, so there is no official warranty; only the shop’s limited promise applies, and any repair will cost extra.
