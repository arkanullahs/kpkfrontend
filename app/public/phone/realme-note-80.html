Why we say that

The Realme Note 80 lives up to its low price with a massive 6.74 inch LCD that refreshes at 90Hz, but the 720p panel only reaches 450 nits, so it can look dim in bright sunlight. The 6300 mAh cell keeps the phone alive for many hours of light use, yet the 15W charger fills it slowly and owners notice the lag when opening apps. The Unisoc T7250 chipset struggles with multitasking and modern games, and the 8MP rear sensor produces soft, detail‑poor images even in good light.

Build quality is solid plastic with an IP54 rating, so it survives drops and dust, and the headphone jack remains useful. Realme UI 5.0 runs Android 15 but includes noticeable bloatware and ads, and you can expect only two or three major OS updates.

How long does the battery really last in daily use?

The 6300 mAh cell can easily last a full day for light users such as messaging, browsing and video streaming, but heavy gaming or video recording will reduce endurance noticeably.

Will this phone handle modern mobile games well?

The Unisoc T7250 chipset is weak for current games. Simple titles may run, but demanding games will show low frame rates and occasional stutter.

Is the camera usable in low‑light conditions?

The 8 MP rear sensor struggles in low light, producing blurry and detail‑poor images. The 5 MP selfie camera has similar limitations, so it is not suitable for night photography.

What warranty and software updates can I expect?

The phone comes with the brand’s official warranty through the listed BD service network. Realme UI 5.0 on Android 15 will receive about two to three major OS updates, but bloatware and ads remain common.
