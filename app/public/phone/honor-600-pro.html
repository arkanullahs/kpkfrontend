Is the Honor 600 Pro worth buying?

What is good

  • Industry-leading 7000 mAh battery endurance
  • Extremely bright 8000 nits AMOLED display
  • Versatile 200MP camera with 3.5x telephoto
  • Premium IP68/69K water and dust resistance

What is not

  • Slow USB 2.0 data transfer speeds
  • Outdated UFS 3.1 storage bottleneck
  • Buggy MagicOS 10 software experience
  • Aggressive paid AI subscription model

Buy it if You prioritize extreme battery life and a high-quality display above all other features.

Skip it if You need fast data transfer speeds, stable software, or a device that does not rely on paid AI subscriptions.

Why we say that

While the 200MP camera system and 3.5x telephoto lens perform well, owners are frustrated by the inclusion of slow USB 2.0 transfer speeds and UFS 3.1 storage in a device at this price point. MagicOS 10 is feature-rich but currently unstable, requiring urgent updates to fix performance issues. Users also dislike the aggressive push toward paid AI subscriptions for features that should be standard.

The design is durable with IP68/69K protection, though many find the aesthetic to be a direct copy of an iPhone. Living with this phone means balancing industry-leading battery endurance against a software experience that feels unfinished and hardware that does not match the premium cost.
