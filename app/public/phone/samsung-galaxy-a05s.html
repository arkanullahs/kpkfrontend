Is the Samsung Galaxy A05s worth buying?

What is good

  • 6.7 inch 90Hz display
  • 50MP main camera
  • 5000 mAh battery with 25W charge
  • headphone jack

What is not

  • Software updates may end soon
  • No 5G support
  • Performance limited for heavy games

Buy it if Buy it if you want a large screen, strong battery and decent camera at a low price.

Skip it if Skip it if you need the latest Android updates or high‑end gaming performance.

Why we say that

The 6.7 inch PLS LCD panel runs at 90Hz, making everyday scrolling feel smooth. The Snapdragon 680 chipset handles basic apps and social media well, but heavy games may lag. The 50MP main sensor captures decent daylight photos, yet low‑light results can be noisy.

Battery life is strong thanks to the 5000 mAh cell and 25W charger, often lasting a full day of mixed use. The phone still carries a headphone jack, which is rare these days. Software runs Android 13 with One UI 7 and promises up to two major Android upgrades, but at three years old the update window is already narrowing.

Owners appreciate the large display and battery, but complain about limited future updates and lack of 5G. Overall it is a solid budget choice for media consumption and casual use.

How long will the battery last on a typical day?

The 5000 mAh battery combined with the efficient Snapdragon 680 can easily last a full day of browsing, social media and video streaming. With moderate use you may even see two days before needing a recharge.

Can the phone handle mobile games smoothly?

The device runs basic games without trouble, but the Snapdragon 680 and 6 GB RAM may struggle with graphics‑intensive titles. Expect lower frame rates or occasional lag in demanding games.

How does the camera perform in low light?

The 50MP sensor with f/1.8 aperture captures reasonable daylight shots, but in low‑light situations the images become noisy and lack detail. Using the night mode can improve results slightly.

Will I receive software updates for this phone?

The Galaxy A05s launched with Android 13 and One UI 7 and is promised up to two major Android upgrades. At three years old the update window is closing, so future updates may be limited.
