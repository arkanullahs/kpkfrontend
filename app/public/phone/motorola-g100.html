Motorola G100 Is it worth buying?

If you need a phone that can run all day without charging, the Motorola G100 delivers that.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You want long battery life, rugged build and decent performance at a mid‑range price.
Consider alternatives if
You need top‑tier gaming, accurate color reproduction or an official brand warranty.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

Review sources ↓

What works well4

  • 7000 mAh battery lasting almost two days
  • IP68/69 rating with military grade protection
  • Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 with smooth daily performance
  • 120 Hz high brightness 6.72 inch display

Reported trade-offs4

  • Colors in photos often oversaturated
  • Camera scores only moderate for price
  • Gaming performance limited to low settings
  • No official manufacturer warranty, only shop warranty

Why we say that

The 7000 mAh battery easily lasts nearly two days for most users, and the 120 Hz high brightness 6.72 inch screen stays visible even in bright sunlight. Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 provides smooth daily performance and can handle light gaming, though demanding titles need low settings. The build is tough with IP68/69 rating and military grade protection, and the phone keeps a headphone jack for wired audio.

The 50 MP Sony LYT600 main camera with OIS records 4K60 video, but colors in photos can be oversaturated and overall camera scores are moderate. Software runs a near stock Hello UI on Android 15 with very low bloat, but Motorola historically offers only one to three major OS upgrades. The only warranty available is from the shop, so verify IMEI and BTRC status before buying.

How long will the battery last on a typical day?

The 7000 mAh cell gives nearly two days of regular use, even with 5G and 120 Hz screen active. Light to moderate usage can stretch it to three days, but heavy gaming will reduce endurance.

Can I play modern games like Genshin Impact smoothly?

The Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 handles daily tasks well and can run Genshin Impact at low settings with 60 fps. High graphics settings will cause frame drops, so expect modest performance for demanding games.

How does the camera perform in low‑light situations?

The 50 MP Sony LYT600 sensor with OIS captures decent low‑light shots, but the overall rating is only 6.8 out of 10. Images may appear a bit oversaturated, and detail can be soft compared to higher end phones.

Will the phone receive regular software updates?

Motorola’s Hello UI is near stock Android 15 and currently has low bloat. Historically the brand provides only one to three major OS upgrades, so long term update support is limited compared with flagship devices.
