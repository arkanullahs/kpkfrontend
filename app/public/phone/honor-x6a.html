Is the Honor X6a worth buying?

What is good

  • Large 5200 mAh battery
  • 50 MP main camera in daylight
  • Headphone jack present
  • 90 Hz display refresh

What is not

  • Dim 720p TFT screen
  • Poor low‑light and selfie photos
  • Laggy performance in common apps
  • Limited future software updates

Buy it if Buy it if you need long battery life, a headphone jack, and are okay with basic performance at a low price.

Skip it if Skip it if you rely on smooth gaming, low‑light photography, or expect many years of Android updates.

Why we say that

The 5200 mAh battery is praised for lasting up to 48 hours in normal use, and the 50 MP main sensor captures decent daylight photos. The phone keeps a headphone jack and a 90 Hz refresh rate, which adds modest comfort. However owners complain about lag in everyday apps like Telegram and Capcut, and gaming is limited to the lowest settings.

The 720p TFT display appears dim and suffers from poor contrast and viewing angles. Low‑light photography is weak and selfies are underwhelming. Software runs on MagicOS 8 with Android 13, but file‑transfer to Windows 11 laptops and NFC availability are unreliable.

At three years old the device is near the end of its update cycle, so future Android upgrades are unlikely.
