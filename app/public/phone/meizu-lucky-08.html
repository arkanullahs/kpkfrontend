Meizu Lucky 08 Is it worth buying?

If you can live without official warranty and accept occasional notification lag, the Meizu Lucky 08 gives flagship‑level screen and huge battery for under ৳28,000.

Review sources ↓

What works well4

  • 6000 mAh battery lasts all day
  • 6.78" LTPO AMOLED 144Hz display
  • Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 smooth daily use
  • 108 MP main camera sharp in daylight

Reported trade-offs4

  • No official manufacturer warranty
  • Notification delays in Flyme OS
  • Poor low‑light photos without OIS
  • No NFC and no Android updates

Why we say that

The 6.78 inch LTPO AMOLED panel runs at 144Hz and reaches 5,000 nits peak, making everyday use feel smooth and bright. The Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 chipset handles typical apps well, though it can get warm during long gaming sessions. The 6,000 mAh cell paired with 45W charging lets you stay unplugged for a full day and a half, which owners rate highly.

The 108 MP main sensor captures sharp photos in good light, but low‑light shots suffer from blur because there is no optical image stabilization. The phone arrives as a gray import, so only the shop’s warranty applies and any repair cost is out of pocket. Software runs Flyme AIOS, which feels fluid but does not receive Android updates and sometimes delays notifications.

Overall it feels like a value‑focused device with some compromises.

How long does the battery really last on a typical day?

The 6,000 mAh cell combined with the LTPO panel lets most users get through a full day of mixed use and still have reserve for evening activities. Owners rate battery life at 8.8 out of 10.

Is the phone good for gaming?

The Snapdragon 7s Gen 2 handles casual games smoothly, but performance drops and heat increase during long sessions. Gaming scores sit around 5.5 out of 10, so it is okay for short play but not for heavy gaming.

Will the camera work well in low light?

The 108 MP sensor is sharp in bright conditions, but the lack of optical image stabilization makes low‑light shots blurry and grainy. Reviewers rate low‑light performance poorly.

What warranty and software support does the phone have?

All units are gray imports, so there is no manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh. The shop provides its own limited warranty and any repair cost is paid by the owner. Flyme AIOS receives only one or two OS updates and Android updates are not provided.
