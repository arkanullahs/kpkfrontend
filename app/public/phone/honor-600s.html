Why we say that

The Honor 600s packs an 8100 mAh cell that owners say easily lasts about 48 hours, and the 6.87‑inch TFT LCD delivers 120 Hz refresh and 1020 nits brightness, looking almost like an AMOLED. Fast wired charging is reported at 80 W, though the spec lists 45 W, and the phone survives drops with an IP69K rating despite its iPhone‑inspired look. Daily tasks feel smooth with Snapdragon 4 Gen 4 and 8 GB RAM, yet high‑end games drop frames and the fingerprint sensor sits low and can be finicky.

MagicOS 10 brings many features but is stuffed with AI‑driven bloatware that many find intrusive. The 50 MP main camera is decent, but the lack of a telephoto lens and poor digital zoom limit versatility. Overall, it offers strong battery and display at a gray‑import price, but the software clutter and warranty gap may deter some buyers.

How long does the battery really last in daily use?

Owners report the 8100 mAh battery easily gives about two days of normal use, even with 5G and a bright screen. Heavy gaming will shorten that time, but most users see roughly 48 hours per charge.

Will this phone handle high‑end mobile games smoothly?

The Snapdragon 4 Gen 4 and 8 GB RAM manage everyday apps well, but reviewers note frame drops in demanding games. It runs cooler than the Pro model, yet high‑end gaming performance is only average.

Is the camera good for low‑light photos and does it have a telephoto lens?

The main sensor is listed as 50 MP and owners describe it as strong, but there is no telephoto lens and digital zoom is weak. Low‑light performance is acceptable, though not exceptional without a dedicated telephoto unit.

What warranty and software updates can I expect?

All units are gray‑import, so there is no manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh. The shop provides its own limited warranty and any repairs are paid. MagicOS 10 runs on Android 16 and typically receives 3‑4 major updates on flagship devices, but heavy bloatware may affect the experience.
