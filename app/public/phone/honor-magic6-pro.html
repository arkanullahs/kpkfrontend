Honor Magic 6 Pro Is it worth buying?

If you want a flagship with a huge, bright screen and fast charging, the Honor Magic 6 Pro delivers that but comes with notable compromises.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You value a large, bright display and flagship speed and are okay with gray‑import warranty limits.
Consider alternatives if

What works well4

  • 6.8‑inch 120 Hz LTPO OLED display
  • 5600 mAh battery with 80 W fast charge
  • Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 flagship performance
  • 50 MP main and selfie cameras

Reported trade-offs4

  • No manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh
  • Daylight video from main camera looks soft
  • Ultrawide struggles in low light
  • Software updates roll out slowly

Why we say that

The 6.8‑inch LTPO OLED panel hits 5000‑nit peaks and runs smooth 120 Hz content, making everyday use feel premium. Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 and 12 GB RAM give you flagship‑level performance in games and apps, and the 5600 mAh cell lasts through a full day with an 80 W wired charge that tops up quickly. Camera quality is mixed: photos are sharp and detailed, but daylight video looks soft, the 2.5× telephoto lags behind rivals, and the ultrawide struggles in low light.

Software runs MagicOS 8 on Android 14, but update rollouts are slow and future updates are uncertain. The phone arrives as a gray import, so there is no official manufacturer warranty; you rely on the shop’s own repair promise, which may involve paid fixes. Overall it feels like a high‑end device with a few practical drawbacks.

How many Android updates will the Honor Magic 6 Pro receive?

The phone ships with Android 14 and MagicOS 8 and promises up to four major OS updates. In practice, owners report that update rollouts are slower than expected, so future updates may arrive later than on some competitors.

What is the battery life and charging speed like?

The 5600 mAh battery provides all‑day usage for most users. It supports 80 W wired fast charging and 66 W wireless charging, allowing a quick top‑up when the battery gets low.

Is the camera good for low‑light and video recording?

Photos are sharp and detailed, but the ultrawide lens is weak in low‑light situations. Daylight video from the main 50 MP sensor appears soft and under‑exposed, and the 2.5× telephoto zoom lags behind many other flagships.
