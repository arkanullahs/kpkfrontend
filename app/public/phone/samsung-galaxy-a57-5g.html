Samsung Galaxy A57 5G Is it worth buying?

If you want a big, bright screen and a solid camera without paying flagship prices, the Galaxy A57 5G can fit the bill.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You value a large vivid display, decent photos and long software support and can accept a gray‑import warranty.
Consider alternatives if
You need top‑tier gaming performance, fast charging or an official Samsung warranty.

What works well4

  • 6.7" Super AMOLED+ 120 Hz display
  • 50 MP main camera with OIS
  • 5000 mAh battery with solid endurance
  • Clean One UI with up to six Android upgrades

Reported trade-offs4

  • Inconsistent OLED panel quality
  • 45 W charging feels slow
  • Heavy games cause heat and lag
  • No wireless charging

Why we say that

The 6.7" Super AMOLED+ panel reaches 1900 nits peak brightness, making outdoor use easy, though some units show inconsistent panel quality. The 50MP main sensor with OIS delivers reliable photos, but it lacks the AI polish of premium models. Performance is smooth for daily tasks, yet heavy gaming brings heat and occasional frame drops.

Battery life is respectable thanks to the 5000 mAh cell, but the 45W charger feels slow compared to newer fast‑charging standards. Software is clean One UI 8.5 with up to six Android upgrades, which is a strong point for longevity. Overall, the phone feels like a trimmed‑down flagship that works well for everyday use but shows its mid‑range limits in gaming, charging speed and panel consistency.

How long does the battery last on a typical day?

The 5000 mAh battery gives solid endurance, easily lasting a full day of moderate browsing, social media and video streaming. Heavy gaming will shorten it, and the 45 W charger refills the pack slower than newer fast‑charging solutions.

Can the Galaxy A57 5G handle heavy gaming?

The Exynos 1680 processor with 8 GB or 12 GB RAM runs everyday apps smoothly, but owners report heat buildup and frame drops in demanding titles. It can manage casual games, yet heavy gaming performance remains limited compared to higher‑end models.

How does the camera perform in low light?

The 50 MP sensor with a 1.56‑inch size, f/1.8 aperture and optical image stabilization captures reliable photos, but it lacks flagship AI enhancements. In low‑light scenes the results are decent, though not as bright or detailed as premium flagships.
