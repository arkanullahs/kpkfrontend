Honor Magic 8 Pro Is it worth buying?

If you want a flagship‑level phone with a dazzling display and huge battery but can live with a plastic back and occasional video softness, the Honor Magic 8 Pro fits the bill.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You want flagship performance, a brilliant display and long battery life and can accept a plastic back and modest video quality.
Consider alternatives if
You need top‑grade video sharpness, a premium glass feel, or a guaranteed manufacturer warranty.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

Review sources ↓

What works well4

  • 6000‑nit peak brightness on LTPO OLED
  • 7100 mAh battery with 100 W fast charging
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 and 12 GB RAM
  • 50 MP main camera with OIS and telephoto

Reported trade-offs4

  • Plastic back feels less premium than glass
  • Video output is soft and lacks sharpness
  • Thermal throttling reduces performance under heavy load
  • Selfie camera lacks autofocus and shows artefacts

Why we say that

The 6.71‑inch LTPO OLED reaches a peak 6000‑nit brightness, making outdoor use effortless, and the 7100 mAh cell earns a 9.3 battery rating, easily lasting a full day of heavy use with 100 W fast charging topping it up in minutes. Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 and 12 GB RAM deliver smooth performance and a 8.4 gaming score, though owners note thermal throttling that can drop speed to about half under prolonged stress. The 50 MP main camera captures excellent stills and low‑light detail, but video appears softer than expected and the 50 MP selfie sensor lacks autofocus, producing artefacts.

MagicOS 10 runs Android 16 with Google services, offering many features but also medium bloat and occasional bugs. Expect three to four major Android updates over the phone’s life.
