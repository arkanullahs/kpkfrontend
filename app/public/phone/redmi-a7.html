Redmi A7 Is it worth buying?

If you need a cheap phone that can make calls, listen to FM and last a day, the Redmi A7 will do, but expect sluggish multitasking and a camera that falls behind its own predecessor.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You need a budget phone for basic calls, messaging and media consumption and can tolerate ads and modest performance.
Consider alternatives if
You plan to run heavy apps, game frequently or rely on a strong camera.

Based on published reviews and owner reports.

Review sources ↓

What works well4

  • Large 6.88 inch display
  • 5200 mAh battery lasts a day
  • Headphone jack and FM radio
  • 15 W charging

Reported trade-offs4

  • Heavy ad bloat in UI
  • Camera worse than A5
  • 120 Hz LCD feels dim
  • Multitasking lags with 3 GB RAM

Why we say that

The 6.88‑inch IPS LCD offers a 120 Hz refresh rate, yet the panel looks dim and the high refresh feels odd on an LCD. The Unisoc T7250 with 3 GB RAM manages basic apps, but heavy apps cause noticeable lag, confirming owners' claim that it is not a multitasking phone. The 13 MP rear sensor is a step back from the A5, delivering average photos even in good light.

Software runs on HyperOS with Android 15 and promises two major upgrades, but users report heavy ad bloat and no Android 16 update despite eligibility. Battery life is decent thanks to a 5200 mAh cell, though charging is limited to 15 W. Overall, it is a budget device with many compromises.
