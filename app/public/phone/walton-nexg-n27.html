Is the Walton NEXG N27 worth buying?

What is good

  • 120Hz display smoothness
  • Large 5000 mAh battery
  • 128GB internal storage
  • Official warranty support

What is not

  • Poor sustained performance
  • Slow 18W charging speed
  • Medium bloatware and ads
  • Low quality display panel

Buy it if Buy this phone only if you need a basic device for light tasks and have a strict budget of 11,999 taka.

Skip it if Skip this phone if you plan to play games or expect a high-quality camera and fast performance.

Why we say that

While the 120Hz display offers a smooth visual experience, owners report that the low-quality IPS In-cell panel limits the overall clarity. The Unisoc T7250 chipset is not designed for heavy tasks, and users frequently complain about poor sustained performance during gaming or multitasking. You will find medium levels of bloatware and ads within the Android 15 Go Edition software.

Charging the 5000 mAh battery is a slow process due to the 18W limit, and the camera quality is rated poorly by current owners. Before purchasing, consider that the newer Walton NEXG N76 is available for 12,499 taka and is generally considered the better buy for a similar price. This phone is best suited for very light tasks, as it is not built to handle demanding applications or long-term heavy usage.
