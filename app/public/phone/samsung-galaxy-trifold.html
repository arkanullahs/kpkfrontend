Is the Samsung Galaxy Z TriFold worth buying?

What is good

  • Top-tier Snapdragon 8 Elite performance
  • Revolutionary 10-inch foldable display
  • Excellent 200MP main camera sensor
  • Native Dex support for multitasking

What is not

  • Extremely heavy 309g weight
  • Rapid battery drain on large screen
  • Inconsistent app scaling
  • No S Pen support

Buy it if Buy this if you are a power user who prioritizes a massive 10-inch screen for gaming and multitasking above all else.

Skip it if Skip this if you need a reliable battery, official brand warranty, or a lightweight device that fits comfortably in your pocket.

Why we say that

While the Snapdragon 8 Elite delivers top-tier performance for gaming and multitasking, the device is physically demanding to own. Owners frequently report that the 309g weight feels like an anchor in their pockets, and the 5600 mAh battery struggles to keep up with the large display, leading to rapid drain under load. The display itself is a highlight with a 9.5 rating, though users must contend with visible creases and inconsistent app scaling.

Because this device is only available through unofficial channels, you lose the security of a brand warranty. You are essentially paying between 469,999 and 474,990 taka for a prototype-like experience that lacks S Pen support and requires careful management of its software quirks.

How long will the Samsung Galaxy Z TriFold receive software updates?

As a flagship device, this phone is eligible for up to 7 years of software updates. It currently runs on Android 16 with One UI 8.5 and features low levels of bloatware and advertisements.

Is the battery life sufficient for heavy gaming?

Owners report that the 5600 mAh battery is insufficient for the 10-inch screen. It drains rapidly under load, especially when using the display at maximum brightness for extended periods.

How does the camera perform for photography?

The 200MP main sensor is flagship-grade and performs well. However, owners note that the secondary lenses are underwhelming given the high price point of the device.
