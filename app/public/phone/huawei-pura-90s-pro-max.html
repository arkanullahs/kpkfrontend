Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max Is it worth buying?

The Pura 90s Pro Max gives you a flagship class camera and a huge battery but struggles with speed and official support.

4 strengths · 4 trade-offs
Choose it if
You prioritize camera, display and battery and can manage workarounds for Google services.
Consider alternatives if

What works well4

  • 50MP camera with excellent low light performance
  • 6.9 inch bright LTPO OLED display
  • 6000mAh battery lasting well over a day
  • Premium metal frame with IP69 protection

Reported trade-offs4

  • No Google Play services at all
  • Kirin 9030S is far slower than modern flagships
  • Charging speed can be inconsistent despite 100W rating
  • Only shop warranty, no official service

Why we say that

The 50MP sensor delivers excellent low light photos and the 6.9 inch LTPO OLED panel is bright and smooth at 120Hz. Battery life easily exceeds a day of mixed use, though the 100W charger may not always hit its peak speed. The build feels premium with a gradient metal frame and IP69 rating.

Software runs EMUI with many preinstalled apps and no Google Play services, so you will need workarounds for many local apps. Performance is modest; the Kirin 9030S falls far behind current flagship chips, making gaming and heavy multitasking feel sluggish. Warranty is limited to the shop’s promise, and any repair cost is out of pocket.

How long does the battery last in daily use?

The 6000mAh battery gives excellent endurance, most users report a full day of mixed use, even with 5G and bright screen. Charging is 100W but speed may vary.

Can I play demanding games smoothly?

The Kirin 9030S lags behind current flagship chips, owner ratings give gaming a 5 out of 10. Expect low frame rates in demanding titles and occasional stutter.

How does the camera perform in low light?

Reviews note industry leading telephoto, variable aperture and exceptional low light dynamic range, giving a 9.5 rating. The 50MP sensor captures detail even at night, though you need to use Huawei’s workarounds for some apps.

All units are gray imports, so there is no manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh. The shop you buy from may offer a limited warranty, but any repair will be paid and official Huawei service centers are not available locally.
