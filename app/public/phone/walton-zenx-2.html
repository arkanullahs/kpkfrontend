Why we say that

The Walton ZENX 2 offers a 6.8 inch IPS LCD panel that runs at 120 Hz, giving smooth scrolling for everyday browsing. Its 5000 mAh battery can easily get you through a day of basic use, but the advertised 100 W fast‑charging never materialises; owners report a full charge taking about three hours. The Unisoc Tiger T615 chipset with 4 GB RAM and 64 GB storage handles simple apps, yet heavy multitasking and modern games lag noticeably.

The 5 MP selfie camera produces acceptable pictures in bright light but fails in low‑light scenes, with limited dynamic range. Android 14 runs a light skin, but software updates are rare and occasional ads appear. Build quality feels durable and the phone includes a headphone jack and FM radio, though there is no IP water‑resistance rating.

Overall it suits users who need a large, rugged screen for basic tasks without expecting premium performance.

How long does the battery last on a typical day?

The 5000 mAh battery provides good endurance for daily tasks such as messaging, browsing and media playback. Users report it easily lasts a full day with moderate use, but heavy gaming will drain it faster.

Can the Walton ZENX 2 run mobile games smoothly?

The phone can handle very light games, but the Unisoc Tiger T615 with 4 GB RAM struggles with demanding titles. Expect noticeable lag and frame drops in graphically intensive games.

Is the camera usable in low‑light conditions?

The 5 MP selfie camera produces acceptable photos in bright light, but owners say it performs poorly in low‑light. Dynamic range and edge detection are weak, resulting in dark and noisy images.

Does the Walton ZENX 2 come with official warranty and software updates?

Yes, the phone is sold through official channels with the brand’s warranty and service network. However, software updates are rare and the light Android skin often shows optimization issues and medium‑level ads.
