Buying verdict

Honor Magic V5 Is it worth buying?

If you want a foldable with flagship performance and a battery that outlasts most rivals, the Honor Magic V5 delivers that but comes with notable compromises.

Choose it if
You need top tier gaming and long battery life in a foldable.

What works well

  • Snapdragon 8 Elite flagship performance
  • 5820mAh battery with class leading endurance
  • 7.95 inch 5000 nits LTPO AMOLED display
  • Fast 66W wired and 50W wireless charging

Reported trade-offs

  • No manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh
  • Bluetooth often drops and needs reboot
  • Screen wobble when unfolded
  • Ultrawide camera produces soft images

Why we say that

The 7.95 inch LTPO AMOLED screen reaches a peak 5000 nits and runs at 120Hz, making bright outdoor use easy. The Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset together with 16GB RAM keeps games like Wuthering Waves smooth at max settings, though the device can get warm near the camera bump. Battery life is class leading thanks to a 5820mAh silicon carbon cell, and 66W wired charging refills it quickly.

Software feels less polished than some rivals, with multitasking bugs and Bluetooth dropping often. The phone arrives as a gray import, so there is no official manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh and any repair will be paid out of pocket. Build is thin and has an IP59 rating, but the inner screen can wobble when opened.

How long does the battery last in everyday use?

The 5820mAh silicon carbon cell gives class leading endurance, lasting significantly longer than the Fold 7. Owner ratings give the battery a 9.4 out of 10, so most users can expect a full day of heavy use without charging.

Can the phone handle high end games without slowing down?

Yes, the Snapdragon 8 Elite processor and 16GB RAM let the phone run games like Wuthering Waves at maximum settings without throttling. Gaming scores are 9.5 and the device stays responsive even when it gets warm near the camera bump.

Is the camera good in low light conditions?

The main 50MP sensor with f/1.6 aperture and OIS performs well in low light, but the ultrawide lens is softer and color tuning can be harsh. Overall camera rating is 7.8, so it is decent but not class leading.
