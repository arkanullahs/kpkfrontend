Is the Honor Magic V5 worth buying?

What is good

  • 7.95 inch foldable LTPO AMOLED display
  • 5820 mAh battery with industry leading endurance
  • Snapdragon 8 Elite performance for gaming
  • 50 MP periscope camera with 3x zoom

What is not

  • Bluetooth drops intermittently
  • MagicOS occasional bugs
  • Camera bump feels wobbly
  • Harsh colour processing

Buy it if Buy it if you want a large foldable with strong performance and can tolerate occasional software quirks.

Skip it if Skip it if you need rock solid Bluetooth stability or natural colour rendering.

Why we say that

The 7.95 inch LTPO AMOLED panel reaches 5,000 nits at peak, making outdoor use easy and the 120Hz refresh feel smooth. The Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset and 16GB RAM keep games and apps running without lag, and the 5,800 mAh battery lasts through a full day of heavy use, outlasting most foldable rivals. The 50MP main camera offers periscope zoom and good low light detail, though colour can look harsh and the camera module may feel a bit wobbly.

MagicOS brings many features but you will see occasional bugs and the Bluetooth connection can cut out. Overall it feels premium, but you need to accept some software quirks and minor hardware oddities to enjoy the large foldable experience.

Owners rate the battery at 9.6 out of 10 and reviewers call the endurance industry leading. The 5,820 mAh cell easily lasts a full day of heavy browsing, video and gaming without needing a recharge.

Is the phone good for gaming?

Yes, the Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset together with 16GB RAM delivers flagship speed. Performance ratings are 9.5 for overall speed and 9.0 for gaming, and sustained performance stays strong under load.

How does the camera perform in low light?

The 50MP sensor with a large 1/1.56 inch size and f/1.6 aperture captures decent low light detail, but reviewers note the colour processing can be harsh and not completely natural.

Will the phone receive long term software updates?

The device ships with Android 15 and MagicOS 10 and is promised up to seven major Android upgrades. Historically Honor flagships get three to four OS updates, so you can expect several years of support.
