Vivo Y18e Is it worth buying?

If you need a large screen and a big battery without spending much, the Vivo Y18e delivers that basic package.

Why we say that

The 6.56 inch IPS LCD runs at 90 Hz and reaches 528 nits, so everyday use feels smooth and visible in bright rooms. The 5000 mAh cell gives decent standby time, but owners note charging is unusually slow. Performance comes from a Helio G85 paired with 4 GB RAM, which handles most apps but struggles with demanding games, reflected in low gaming scores.

The 13 MP main sensor captures acceptable photos in good light, yet low light results are weak. There is no fingerprint reader and the phone arrives as a gray import, meaning only the shop’s warranty applies and any repair costs are out of pocket. Software runs Android 14 with Funtouch 14, but budget models typically receive only two major updates and include medium level bloat.

How long does the battery last on a typical day?

The phone uses a 5000 mAh battery which gives decent standby and can last most of a day with moderate use, but heavy browsing or gaming will drain it faster and the slow charging means you need several hours to refill.

Can I play games smoothly on this phone?

The device runs a Helio G85 with 4 GB RAM and a 90 Hz screen, which is enough for casual titles, but demanding games feel laggy and the gaming rating is low, so performance is limited.

How good is the camera in low light?

The main camera is a 13 MP sensor with an f/2.2 aperture, which produces acceptable photos in bright conditions, but owners report that low light images are dark and lack detail.

Will I get an official warranty and how many Android updates?

All listings are gray imports, so there is no official manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh; the shop provides its own limited warranty and any repair cost is paid by the owner. Budget models usually receive only two major Android updates.
