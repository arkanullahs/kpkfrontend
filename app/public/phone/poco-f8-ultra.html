Is the POCO F8 Ultra worth buying?

What is good

  • Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 performance
  • 6500 mAh battery lasts long
  • 50 MP main camera with OIS and 5× tele
  • 100 W fast charging

What is not

  • No official warranty in Bangladesh
  • Heavy CPU throttling under sustained load
  • Ultrawide and selfie cameras lack autofocus
  • Software updates are very slow

Buy it if If you want flagship‑level speed, massive battery life and fast charging and can live with a shop‑provided gray‑import warranty, this phone fits.

Skip it if If you need reliable software updates, smooth third‑party launcher gestures or a strong selfie camera, look elsewhere.

Why we say that

Its Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset delivers top‑tier performance and scores 9.6 in owner tests, making gaming and heavy apps feel smooth until the chip heats to about 41 °C and throttles. The 6.9‑inch AMOLED screen is bright with a 3500‑nit peak, yet it lacks LTPO control so the 120 Hz refresh is always on, draining power. The 50 MP main sensor with OIS and 5× telephoto captures good low‑light shots, while the ultrawide and 32 MP selfie lenses miss autofocus.

HyperOS 3 runs Android 16 but updates are slow, bloat is high and third‑party launcher gestures are blocked. Build quality is solid with IP68 rating and a 2.1‑channel speaker, but the lack of official warranty means repairs are paid and you must verify IMEI status before buying.

GSMArena Official · Poco F8 Ultra review ↗

GSMArena found the POCO F8 Ultra to be a highly competitive flagship that breaks the mold with its unconventional denim design and innovative built-in subwoofer. They praised the excellent battery life, top-tier processor performance, and dependable main camera. However, they noted some severe CPU throttling in stress tests and criticized the frustrating lack of autofocus on the unusually narrow ultrawide lens.

Tech Spurt · POCO F8 Ultra Review | Now THIS is an upgrade! ↗

Tech Spurt loved the aggressive upgrades, noting that the addition of eSIM, a massive battery increase, and a bold denim design make it a true ultra-tier device. While highly impressed by the raw gaming performance and class-leading Bose speakers, he criticized the camera's tendency to blur moving subjects like pets and found the software far too obsessed with imitating iOS design elements.

Adam Lobo TV · POCO F8 Ultra After 3 Months: ❌ I Was WRONG ↗

Returning to the phone after three months, Adam Lobo admitted he was wrong to initially hold back his recommendation, as the unique denim back stayed completely clean and the Bose audio remained unmatched. Despite ongoing annoyances like a wildly unpredictable battery drain when dropping below ten percent and a practically useless macro mode, he concluded the device heavily rewards users who care most about elite gaming and sound quality.

TechEdit · 2026’s Best Value Phone? | POCO F8 Ultra 7-Month Review ↗

TechEdit declared the phone an absolute steal at its current price, offering features that comfortably outclass far more expensive 2026 flagships. They highlighted the stellar combination of a giant battery, a powerhouse processor, and highly capable log video recording for creators, making it very easy to overlook its heavy operating system and a notably restrictive ultrawide camera.

How long does the battery really last in daily use?

The 6500 mAh battery can give around 12 hours of screen‑on time according to owners, and the 100 W charger refills it in about half an hour. Heavy gaming will reduce that time noticeably.

Is the POCO F8 Ultra good for gaming?

Gaming scores 8.4 in owner ratings and the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 provides high peak power. However, sustained sessions raise the temperature to about 41 °C and cause CPU throttling, which can lower frame rates.

How does the camera perform in low light?

The 50 MP main sensor with f/1.7 aperture and OIS captures decent low‑light photos, and the 5× telephoto works well. The ultrawide lens is weak and lacks autofocus, and the 32 MP selfie camera also misses autofocus in dim conditions.

Will I get official warranty and regular updates?

All listings are gray‑import units, so there is no manufacturer warranty in Bangladesh. The shop may offer its own limited warranty, but repairs are paid. HyperOS updates are delayed, with only two to three major OS upgrades expected.
