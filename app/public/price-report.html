Bangladesh phone price report

We read the phone shops of Bangladesh every day or two and write down what they are asking. This page is what 56 of those readings say about the market itself — not about which phone to buy, but about what a price here actually behaves like.

2,391 handsets, 17 shops, 56 nights of readings between 11 Jun 2026 and 11 Sep 2026. Nobody else in this market publishes these numbers, so nobody else can check them either — which is why every rule they obey is written down at the bottom.

A report is only as good as its date: this edition was compiled from shop readings taken up to 11 Sep 2026.

What the official set costs over the import

68 handsets across 13 brands are on sale here in both channels at the same RAM and storage, which is the only comparison worth making: the raw cheapest-official against cheapest-import difference on a Z Fold 6 is 143%, and most of that is 512GB against 256GB. On the same box the official unit costs a median of ৳10,999 more.

Two ways to put that as a percentage, and they are not interchangeable:

Which percentage, and of what

  • Premium over the import price — median 21%. (official − import) ÷ import. What the official unit adds to what the import asks.
  • Saving against the official price — median 18%. (official − import) ÷ official. How much of the official price the import takes off.

This is the channel gap, not the price of the warranty. Holding the configuration takes storage and RAM out of it; duty, the distributor’s margin, the shop’s margin and after-sales are all still inside, and nothing in this data says how the total splits. The eight widest in taka:

How reliably the stock holds

Of the 434 handsets we have recorded on at least 20 nights and that a shop still carries, 225 had somebody holding stock on every single night we looked. Another 207 went missing on fewer than one night in ten. Only 2 disappeared more often than that.

Which is a duller answer than “which models keep selling out” expects. Read it narrowly: it describes what these sellers reported about these handsets, over the nights we recorded them. A stock flag is a shop’s own label and not a checkout we completed; handsets delisted during the window are excluded, which removes the ones most likely to have gone missing; and shops we do not read are not in it at all. The denominator is nights we recorded that handset, never “days”.

Shops appear as Shop 01, Shop 02 and so on, and the shop-to-shop file names none at all. This site does not publish which shop is asking which price for a named handset, and a download link is not the place to start. The aliases are stable across a build, which is what they are needed for: checking that twelve shops in a total are twelve distinct shops.

How these numbers are made

The rules every figure above obeys

  • Like for like where we can hold the axes. The channel, shop and storage comparisons above each hold every axis but the one they ask about: official against official, import against import, same configuration, same shop. Where an axis cannot be held — the movement figures, next bullet — the page says so instead of claiming it was.
  • Nights we looked, never days. We know what the shops published on the nights our read succeeded. A night we did not read is not a night a price held.
  • Across configurations, not like for like. Our price history stores one number per channel per night and not one per configuration. So a cheap 128GB listing selling out reads as a rise, and a cheaper one appearing reads as a fall, on a phone whose configurations all held their price. Every movement figure here is the lowest asking price on a channel and is labelled as that. It is not a like-for-like price cut, which would need a per-variant history we do not keep.
  • Rises are not published. Not because they survive that objection — they do not, and neither do falls — but because a fall points at something you can go and check today, and a rise built the same way reads as news about a manufacturer price and is not one.
  • Noise floors, stated, and chosen by us. A move under 4% or 700 taka is dropped, a spread needs three distinct shops, a shop needs 25 comparisons before it is ranked, and a storage step needs eight observations. These are reporting thresholds we picked to keep coincidences out; they are not tests of statistical adequacy, and a figure that clears them can still be thin.
  • We have not held any of these phones. This is a record of what Bangladeshi shops published, nothing more. How the rest of the site works.
